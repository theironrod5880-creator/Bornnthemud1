# Chapter 2: The Name Jehovah

A year passed. 

The ultimatum—*"God, whoever you are, you show me who you are, and I'll serve you"*—had been buried under the daily grind of prison survival. The man had not kept his end of the bargain. The guilt of it was a constant, heavy companion. 

Then came April 2001. He found himself back inside, locked down in a cell in Block A. But this time, the dynamic was different. 

His new cellmate was a quiet man. He wasn't loud. He didn't run with the prison politics. He just sat on his bunk and read the Bible. The man knew him to be a disfellowshipped member of a Bible study group—shunned by his former congregation, carrying his own quiet sorrow. 

The man had a secret. He had fashioned a makeshift weapon—a knife. In the dangerous ecosystem of the prison, it was a necessity. But during a lockdown, an instinct of respect kicked in. The cellmate had done nothing to him. The man pulled the knife out, looked at the quiet reader, and flushed it. He watched relief wash over the older man's face.

The man decided to use the cellmate for a different kind of target practice. He wanted to humiliate him. He wanted to prove that this quiet Bible reader was wrong. The man was steeped in the prison faith of Odinism, but he still carried the fragments of his childhood. He believed Jesus was God. 

He walked over to the cellmate, looked at the pages, and sneered a challenge:

*"Where do you get the name Jehovah from? I thought Jesus was God."*

The cellmate didn't argue. He didn't raise his voice. He simply turned the pages of his King James Version. He stopped at **Psalm 83:18**.

He handed the Bible to the man, or perhaps just held it up so he could look over his shoulder. The man’s eyes locked onto the text. In the archaic English of the King James, the verse read:

> *"That men may know that thou, whose name alone is JEHOVAH, art the most high over all the earth."*

Time stopped.

It wasn't just a theological fact. It was a personal address. The name was there. It was real. And as the man stared at the letters, a memory tore through him like lightning. 

It was the night in the cell a year earlier. The ultimatum. The desperate cry: *"God, whoever you are, you show me who you are."*

He had asked for a name. And the God of the universe had just given it to him. Not through a burning bush. Not through a vision. But in a concrete cell, through the words of a shunned man, in a book that had been preserved for thousands of years. 

The man grabbed the edge of the bunk. Tears rolled down his face. 

He now knew who God was. The Creator of the universe had a name. It was Jehovah. 

From that split second forward, nobody could ever change his mind. The religious fog, the prison politics, the confusion—it all burned away. He had made a promise, and even though he was failing to keep it, God had kept His part. The Creator had answered the ultimatum of a broken man.
