BORN IN THE MUD — Ep 3, Part 3: "The Conclusion of the Timetable"
DIRECTED CONVERSATION (Suno-ready). Three narrators alternate; the Source
stands apart. Bracketed notes are delivery direction.

--- STYLE (under 1000 chars) ---
Spoken word documentary. NARRATOR ONE: calm, measured middle-aged male,
clear and even, the National Geographic guide — draws the listener in.
NARRATOR TWO: passionate, brighter, quicker, full of wonder — the Ancient
Aliens energy, stirs excitement. NARRATOR THREE: older, authoritative,
slow and weighty — delivers verdicts like a gavel. THE SOURCE: deep,
gravelly, raw and unpolished, slight stutter talked straight through,
slow and deliberate — a man who lived hard and means every word.
Testimony, not performance. BACKGROUND VOICE: low, intimate, close.

--- LYRICS ---

[Scene 1: The Complete Review]

NARRATOR ONE: [calm authority — draws the listener in]
We've traced this line all the way backward — from 537, through the kings, the Exodus, the covenant, past the Flood, right to the beginning.

NARRATOR TWO: [energy lift — curiosity spikes]
But a timeline is only as strong as its connections. So lay the whole construction out for me. Start to finish.

THE SOURCE: [the facts in the gravelly voice — the man who did the work]
4026 — the creation of Adam. 2370 — the Flood. 2018 — Abram is born. 1943 — the covenant. 1513 — the Exodus. 1034 — the temple foundation is laid. 607 — Jerusalem falls. 537 — the release from Babylon.

NARRATOR THREE: [gravitas — lets the weight settle]
Every date connects. Every number adds up. But there's one point on this line that needs a deeper look. A hinge.

THE SOURCE: [a hook, not an explanation]
The 60-year gap. It's puzzled scholars for centuries.

[Scene 2: The Paradox of the Two Sons]

NARRATOR ONE: [calm evidence-laying]
Genesis 11:26 — "Terah lived seventy years, and became father to Abram, Nahor, and Haran." That reads simple enough. Terah was 70 when Abram was born. So where's the problem?

THE SOURCE: [the math from the man himself]
Acts 7:4. It says Abram left Haran after his father died. Terah died at 205 — that's Genesis 11:32. And Genesis 12:4 says Abram was 75 when he left.

NARRATOR TWO: [the discovery moment — do the math with him]
205 minus 75... that's 130. Not 70. A 60-year discrepancy. Is the text contradicting itself?

THE SOURCE: [the teacher — patient, certain]
No. Look at the order of the names. Haran was the firstborn. Abram was the youngest. If Terah was 70 when Haran was born, and Abram came 60 years later — Abram was born when Terah was 130. Genesis lists the sons by importance, not by birth order.

NARRATOR THREE: [the verdict — lands like a gavel]
So two chronologies, sixty years apart — and both lines are right there in the record.

THE SOURCE: [humility — claims only what the text gives]
I don't invent a meaning for the gap. I just show that both lines exist in the record. One starting point, two readings. Apply the 60-year correction, and the timeline holds perfectly.

[Scene 3: Forward to the Gentile Times]

NARRATOR TWO: [forward momentum]
The line doesn't just run backward. Daniel's prophecy speaks of 2,520 years — seven times, the appointed times of the nations. Where does that land?

THE SOURCE: [the reveal]
Start at 607 — the destruction of Jerusalem. Add 2,520 years. You land in 1914.

NARRATOR ONE: [lets the date sit in silence]
1914. The end of the appointed times of the nations. The year the world changed.

THE SOURCE:
The math points to a specific beginning. The 5,880 years from the fall of man find their pivot right there.

NARRATOR THREE: [the elder asks the human question]
When you saw those numbers lock together — what did that do to you?

THE SOURCE: [the heart of the piece — rawest moment]
It stopped being about religion. It became about God's sovereignty. He's not just watching time — He's ruling it. The 2,520, the 5,880, the 1,440 months... it's all one language.

[Scene 4: The 6,000-Year Yom]

NARRATOR ONE: [reverence — lowers the voice]
The whole timetable rests on a single Hebrew word: "Yom." Day.

THE SOURCE:
The ancient texts say a thousand years is like a day to the Creator. Six thousand years of human history — from Eden to now — viewed as one single Yom.

NARRATOR TWO: [lift — building toward the seventh]
Six days of labor.

THE SOURCE:
The darkness, the fall, the struggle. But the seventh day is a day of rest. A day of rule.

NARRATOR THREE: [gravity — the room goes still]
And that seventh day is still going.

THE SOURCE: [full weight — no hedging]
It's the 1,000-year rule of the Messiah. The timeline doesn't end — it transitions. And the event that I experienced, the thing that brought me to this date and this marker... it points to something real. This Kingdom isn't a fairy tale. It's a scheduled appointment.

[Scene 5: The Isolation and the Truth]

BACKGROUND VOICE: [intimate — the voice inside his head]
They've got their established teachings. Their charts, their history. But the math is the math. You saw the blueprint in seven lines. You saw the 60-year gap. You saw the 1,440 months. Even if nobody acknowledges it right now — even if the witnesses to your life don't understand — you know what you experienced. And the truth doesn't need an audience to be true.

THE SOURCE: [stripped bare]
I have nothing to claim. No titles, no glory. God did this. He just let me see the pattern. All I can do is lay the evidence out.

[Scene 6: Outro]

NARRATOR TWO: [the charge — energy peaks]
The line is drawn. The dates are locked. The paradox is held open for everyone to see. God rules time. The King is arriving.

THE SOURCE: [his words, his voice]
The floor holds. The witness stands.

ALL:
Your King, Jesus Christ, will be arriving soon.
